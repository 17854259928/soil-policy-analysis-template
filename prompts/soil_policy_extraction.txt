Task: extract and analytically classify one bounded fragment from an official
soil, agricultural, land-use or environmental policy document.

1. Policy identity
- Record the official policy, programme, intervention or legal-act name.
- Record legal identifiers, jurisdiction, responsible authority and parent
  programme only when stated.
- Distinguish adoption, entry into force, implementation and target dates.

2. Soil-policy relevance
- Assign DIRECT only when soil protection, restoration, monitoring or soil
  condition is an explicit objective.
- Assign EXPLICIT_MECHANISM when the primary objective is different but the
  document explicitly states a soil practice, pathway, indicator or effect.
- Assign CO_BENEFIT_ONLY when soil improvement is explicitly secondary.
- Assign NOT_DEMONSTRATED when a soil relationship is merely plausible.
- Do not equate climate action, sustainability or good agricultural practice
  with soil-policy relevance without local textual evidence.

3. Objectives, threats and instruments
- Apply only codes defined in the supplied soil-policy ontology.
- Preserve the explicit name of an instrument even when no controlled code is
  suitable.
- Distinguish binding regulation, support conditionality, voluntary payments,
  result-based payments, investment, advice, monitoring and certification.
- Record target actors and bindingness.

4. Implementation and coverage
- Extract implementation start/end, geographic coverage, eligible land,
  farming systems, target groups, budget, currency and funding period.
- Record participating farms, supported area and adoption rate only when stated.
- Do not convert a planned target into an achieved output.

5. Soil practices and indicators
- Extract only explicitly named management practices.
- For every soil indicator, preserve value, unit, direction, baseline, target
  year, soil depth, sampling basis, spatial scale and measurement method when
  observable.
- Keep measured, modelled and expected values separate.

6. Results and effects
- Separate implementation inputs, adoption outputs, measured soil outcomes,
  modelled outcomes and broader effects.
- Record GHG, water, biodiversity, production, cost, income or food-security
  effects only when explicitly reported or expected in the source.
- Label causal language as explicit, expected, reported, modelled or inferred.

7. Evidence and missing information
- Every positive or uncertain label requires a concise verbatim evidence cue
  and page, section, table, row or other available locator.
- Do not calculate, aggregate or harmonise values unless explicitly requested.
- Unknown scalar fields are null; unknown arrays are empty.
- Report contradictions, ambiguous terminology and missing units as warnings.

8. Output
- Return exactly one JSON object matching the supplied schema.
- Do not add Markdown fences, comments, prefixes or fields not in the schema.
